# Manafa Digital Business Onboarding Presentation

## Purpose

This is Manafa’s reusable company and Digital Business orientation for Business Analyst and UX/UI co-op joiners.

- **Owner:** Product Director, Digital Business, or a delegated team member
- **Audience:** BA and UX/UI co-ops, commonly from MIS or IS backgrounds
- **Language:** English first
- **Format:** Interactive 16:9 HTML presentation
- **Expected duration:** Approximately 60–90 minutes, depending on discussion
- **Visual direction:** Manafa’s Product Leadership system, informed by the earlier *Welcome to Manafa* presentation
- **Boundary:** This is an onboarding presentation, not the Buddy Program or the seven-month training plan

The presentation explains the company, business, people, products, stakeholders, Digital Business scope, platform direction and daily working environment. Individual learning courses, practical assignments, buddy responsibilities and monthly development targets remain separate.

## Intended outcome

After the session, a new joiner should be able to explain:

- What Manafa does and how it connects businesses with capital
- The difference between borrowers and investors, and the SCF-specific roles of buyers and funders
- Manafa’s key company, regulatory, investment and national-program milestones
- Manafa’s seven financing products and the lifecycle behind a financing request
- The executive team, Manafa group companies and key external partners
- Which internal departments Digital Business works with
- Where Digital Product sits and who belongs to the Digital Business team
- What Digital Business owns across channels and back-office products
- Why Manafa is moving away from one monolithic Admin Portal
- The target channel, core-hub, shared-library and embedded structure
- How a demand moves from Digital Factory to a production release
- The core collaboration tools and company essentials used day to day

## Slide structure

### Opening

1. **Welcome to Manafa**
   Digital Business onboarding.

### Chapter 1 — Manafa

2. **Manafa**
   Section break: the company, its business and the people behind it.

3. **Manafa at a glance**
   A concise definition of Manafa through three ideas: business financing, capital participation and the connected operation behind both.

4. **How Manafa connects capital with business needs**
   One connected visual showing borrowers and investors, together with the buyer and funder roles used specifically in Supply Chain Financing. The slide avoids presenting the business lines as separate companies or disconnected platforms.

5. **How Manafa grew into a national financing platform**
   A visual company timeline covering foundation, regulation, Series A, Saudi Unicorns, national SCF programs and the SCF Regulatory Sandbox.

6. **Two regulatory milestones shaped the business**
   The 2022 Debt Crowdfunding Licence and the 2025 SCF Regulatory Sandbox permission from the Saudi Central Bank.

7. **Investment and national recognition accelerated the journey**
   The SAR 106 million Series A led by STV and Wa’ed Ventures, and Manafa’s selection for the Saudi Unicorns Program, each with a short explanation of why it mattered.

8. **SCF partnerships moved Manafa to national scale**
   Aramco at FII8 in 2024 and Saudi Electricity Company at FII9 in 2025, presented together as national-scale SCF milestones.

9. **Manafa today**
   Current public achievement figures: total financing, users, team members and partnerships. These figures must be revalidated before each cohort.

10. **The executive team**
    Abdulaziz Aladwani as Founder and CEO, followed by the confirmed executive team with aligned portraits, names and titles.

11. **A growing group with shared roots**
    Manafa, Sukuk Capital, Abyan Capital and Manafa Technologies. Manafa Technologies is presented as the technology-delivery company in Pakistan using the Manafa identity rather than a team photograph.

12. **The ecosystem around Manafa**
    STV and Wa’ed Ventures as investors, followed by the strategic-partner logos: Kafalah, Mudad, SME Bank, Cultural Development Fund, Tourism Development Fund and SIDF.

### Chapter 2 — Business and products

13. **Business and products**
    Section break: the financing portfolio and the journey behind every request.

14. **Seven ways Manafa finances businesses**
    One visual portfolio of Invoice Financing, Letter of Guarantee Financing, Purchase Order Financing, Working Capital Financing, Payroll Financing, Real Estate Financing and Supply Chain Financing. Embedded lending is not shown as a financing product.

15. **What happens from application to closure**
    A clearly explained six-stage lifecycle: company onboarding; financing request; credit assessment; offer and approval; funding and disbursement; servicing and closure. The stages are visually tagged by business, Manafa and capital participation.

### Chapter 3 — Digital Business

16. **Digital Business**
    Section break: the stakeholders, team, platforms and delivery model.

17. **The departments we work with**
    Credit; Compliance, Risk & BCM; Legal & Governance; Business Operations; Portfolio Management; Collections; Customer Care; Technology & Engineering; Cybersecurity; Finance & Treasury; Human Resources; Business Development & Partnership; Marketing & Branding Communication; Digital Investors; Digital Experience; and Internal Audit.

18. **Where Digital Product sits**
    Product reporting to CTO Mohammed Shahram Javid, with Abdullah Almazyad leading Digital Business and Raghad leading Digital Investors & Digital Experience.

19. **Meet the Digital Business team**
    The current Product Leads, Business Analysts and UX/UI Designers on one team slide, with Abdullah shown once as the department leader.

20. **What Digital Business owns**
    The business-side channels—Borrower, Buyer, Funder and Embedded—and the back-office product domains—Customer, Lending, Counterparty and Financial.

21. **Why the current product model must change**
    The present challenge of one growing Admin Portal: unclear ownership, demand competition, cross-domain coupling and increasing scale complexity.

22. **How the target structure was developed**
    The strategy method: internal stakeholder discovery, external market benchmarking, then testing and refinement. The output is a domain-led suite with explicit ownership.

23. **From one Admin Portal to a modular product suite**
    The target layered model:

    - **Channels:** Borrower, Investor, Funder and Buyer
    - **Core Hubs:** Customer, Lending, Counterparty and Financial
    - **Shared Library:** Identity & Access, Data & Insights, Knowledge Management and Logs
    - **Embedded:** a separate Embedded Channel and Embedded Hub

    The slide must state that the transition is in progress and not every component is live.

24. **The transition is already underway**
    Current portfolio status: CRM, Invoice Management and SCF FinOps as live or in delivery; Client Lifecycle, Buyer Management, Funder Management, LOS, and Loan Management & Collections across design, BA or ideation.

25. **How a demand becomes a production release**
    Digital Factory demand; discovery; a Confluence BRD for large or complex solutions; linked delivery work in the relevant Jira project; release, validation and learning. The demand, analysis and delivery tasks remain traceable.

26. **How we work together**
    The feature workflow from request through UAT and go-live, alongside the recurring team rhythm: team meetings, sprint grooming and direct feedback.

27. **Your daily toolkit and company essentials**
    Slack, Microsoft 365, Jira, Confluence and Jisr. The slide also distinguishes the professional certificate-support policy and approved certificate list from the free or low-cost learning courses used in the training journey.

### Closing

28. **Welcome aboard**
    Questions and a concise closing message.

- Staging access instructions and test data
- Seven-product creation, credit-assessment, investment and closure exercises
